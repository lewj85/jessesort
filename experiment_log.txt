- Section 2 is the only authoritative active roadmap. Historical "next steps" inside

TIMING CONVENTION
- Unless stated otherwise, candidate/baseline ratio <1 means faster and >1 means slower.
- Small effects require evidence proportional to their size. Under E077/E100 methodology,
  use the canonical paired population when possible; effects near the noise floor should
  use more trials and/or a phase-isolated, layout-matched causal harness.
- For narrow-phase changes, report BOTH local phase impact and whole-sort impact. Do not
  reject a large local improvement solely because Amdahl's law dilutes the total effect.
- When a strong, repeatable phase-local win conflicts with a small whole-sort regression,
  treat the disagreement as UNRESOLVED rather than immediately retaining/rejecting the
  change. Increase trial counts substantially on BOTH measurements, preserve the canonical
  input population, and use fork-isolated whole-sort timing when allocator/process-state
  effects are plausible. E090 and the high-trial E102 revalidation both showed that an
  under-sampled whole-sort signal can reverse after oversampling while the local win remains.
- Rejection taxonomy (E103):
    * MECHANISM REJECTED: the targeted phase itself loses materially with adequate trials;
      do not reopen unchanged without a materially new mechanism or hardware regime.
    * INTEGRATION REJECTED: the local kernel/mechanism wins, but the attempted way of
      feeding work into it or amortizing its overhead loses; the mechanism remains open
      to a different integration design.
    * POLICY / STALE-DEPENDENT REJECTED: a threshold, scheduler, or route loses under the
      then-current cost model; revalidate after major changes to the underlying kernel or
      phase balance before treating the policy as permanently closed.
- E103 mandatory disagreement rule: when a strong repeatable local win conflicts with a
  small whole-sort loss or neutral result, status is UNRESOLVED. Substantially increase
  trials on BOTH layers, preserve the same canonical seed population, and use fork-isolated
  whole-sort timing when process/allocator state can bias order. Do not let a lightly sampled
  end-to-end signal overrule a high-confidence causal phase win.
- E103 mandatory propagation rule: after RETAINING any algorithmic/performance change in
  one variation, immediately audit V1-V7 for applicability before moving on to unrelated
  optimization work. For every other variation, record one of: tested+retained, tested+rejected,
  not applicable (with architectural reason), or intentionally different (with reason). Do not
  silently assume shared code means propagation, and do not leave plausible propagation as an
  untracked future thought; add it to the active roadmap if it cannot be tested immediately.
- Never calibrate against a std::sort result from another run/VM/session. Use same-run
  paired evidence because host state, compiler layout, allocator reuse, and scheduling
  can move absolute timings materially.

1.1 BENCHMARK AND AUDIT STANDARD
- E077 is the current whole-sort benchmark methodology; E100 centralizes its seed policy.
- Canonical `make run` / `make run500` / `make bench500` profile:
    * C++20, -O3, -march=native, -DNDEBUG
    * n = {1k, 10k, 100k, 1m}
    * 2 untimed warmups per workload
    * 500 measured paired trials at 1k/10k/100k
    * 50 measured paired trials at 1m for the all-size sweep
    * one deterministic unique input seed per randomized trial
- MANDATORY POST-CHANGE ACCEPTANCE RULE: after every algorithmic/performance code change, run the
  full canonical benchmark above across ALL SEVEN variations (V1-V7), even when the code change
  directly targets only one variation. Use the benchmark's normal persisted-results resume handling
  after shell/tool timeouts; resume the SAME run directory until the sweep is complete.
- Every performance-change evaluation must display/preserve all seven generated ratio tables. Narrow
  A/B or phase-local harnesses are diagnostic only and do not replace this full-suite acceptance run.
- When a complete canonical resumed sweep conflicts with a smaller ad-hoc/end-to-end timing sample,
  treat the canonical sweep as authoritative for repository-level retention decisions. Use the narrow
  harness only to explain mechanism, not to overrule the canonical result by itself.
- Targeted numeric-size invocations use the requested trial count directly; they do not
  automatically apply the 1m 10:1 reduction.
- Every algorithm and std::sort receive the exact same source vector within a trial.
  Execution order rotates by trial and reverses every eight-trial block. Correctness
  validation is outside timed regions.
- Canonical seed policy for normal benchmarks and one-off A/B tests:
    * base seed: `jessesort::bench::kCanonicalBaseSeed` (0x8A5CD789)
    * mixer: `jessesort::bench::trial_seed()` in `benchmarks/benchmark_seed.h`
    * do not invent a new seed population for an ordinary one-off test; explicitly label
      intentional held-out/confirmation cohorts instead.
- Preserve raw paired timings. Report robust statistics such as median, p25/p75, IQR,
  MAD, ratio of medians, and median paired ratio as appropriate; do not silently trim
  outliers.
- For header-only/template-heavy close A/B tests, prefer a same-binary or phase-isolated
  causal harness. E099 showed structured workloads can exhibit large first/second-sort
  allocator/process-state effects; fork-isolated paired children are appropriate when
  ordinary same-process alternation remains biased.
- Routine workload families: Random, Sorted, Reverse, Sorted+Noise(5%), Random%100,
  Alternating, Sawtooth, BlockSorted, OrganPipe, and Rotated.
- `Rotated` intentionally keeps a randomized split. E100 audited 500 canonical 100k
  trials: mean split/discontinuity position 49.322%, median 49.954%, decile counts close
  to uniform, and exactly 250/500 positions before versus at/after the nominal 50%
  freeze decision point. Keep the randomized generator; control it through canonical
  seeds rather than deterministic stratification.
- Benchmark runs are resumable with `./benchmark <trials> <size|all> <warmups>
  <existing_run_dir>` and reconstruct summaries only from complete paired trials.
- Validate workload generators rather than trusting labels. Test non-integer types and
  custom comparators separately when genericity/correctness/performance conclusions
  depend on value representation.
- Do not assume an optimization transfers between variations; propagation gets its own
  causal evidence unless the change is purely mechanical/documentary.

V1. ACTUAL PHYSICAL PILES
- Header: `include/jessesort/v1_actual_piles.h`
- Namespace: `jessesort::actual_piles`
- Representation: vector-backed physical patience piles with separate tail mirrors.
- Early routing: equality preserves the current game. A >=32-element monotone prefix is
  materialized directly. Otherwise the true first-64 probe routes Random-like inputs to
  direct bit-walk tail search, high-locality inputs to exact previous-pile hint + binary
  fallback, and ambiguous inputs to ordinary binary search (E046/E047/E056).
- E099 natural-run route: after an initial monotone prefix covering >=12.5% of n, later
  strict runs of length >=8 whose endpoints resolve to one physical pile are appended in
  bulk. This is retained and especially effective for OrganPipe/Rotated.
- E109 convergence baseline: V1 now uses the same non-freezing Random merge predicate as V2/V3:
  the first-64 `earlyRandomLike` signal + common 3.5/4.25/5.0 pile-density thresholds + the
  <=96-byte trivially-copyable type gate. The older V1-only minority-game balance predicate is
  no longer part of the common starting policy.
- Merge kernel: E083 raw pointers + E085 bidirectional low/high-end branchless merge are retained.
- E111 removed selective PowerSort from the common V1/V2/V3 policy after cross-variation revalidation.
- E115 consolidates V1/V2/V3 onto one shared ends-based adjacent-pair post-flatten merge driver with
  adaptive ordered-boundary probing and the retained E045/E085 routing/kernel choices.

V2. SIMULATED BLUEPRINT
- Header: `include/jessesort/v2_simulated.h`
- Namespace: `jessesort::simulated`
- Representation: sentinel-free value tails plus packed 32-bit blueprint tags.
- Early insertion routing: >=32 monotone-prefix recognition; first-64 structure signal;
  one-time no-hint continuation for sustained Random-like structure; split exact-hint
  path for >=75% hint locality; ordinary search otherwise (E043/E065).
- E098 natural-run route: if the initial strict monotone prefix covers >=12.5% of n,
  later strict runs of length >=8 are endpoint-tested and blueprint-tagged in bulk when
  both endpoints map to the same pile. Exact blueprint/count/tail equality was required
  in A/B validation.
- Reconstruction: descending-game runs first, ascending-game runs second. E081 normalizes
  packed local tags to global run IDs before scatter. E086 keeps an explicit `step[]`
  table below 500k and derives direction from normalized run ID at >=500k, where the
  metadata-stream reduction wins.
- E109 convergence baseline replaces V2's old pre-E085 E053 density coefficients with the shared
  non-freezing 3.5/4.25/5.0 thresholds. V2 keeps its `earlyRandomLike` prerequisite, E045 gallop
  selector, and adaptive ordered-boundary probing. E101 previously removed the obsolete E063 per-pair
  imbalance fallback. E111 removed selective PowerSort; E115 moves V2 onto the same shared ends-based
  adjacent-pair post-flatten merge driver used by V1/V3.
- High-entropy merge: E082 raw pointers + E085 bidirectional branchless merging retained.
  E092 widened the merge-only trivially-copyable type cutoff through 96 bytes; 128 bytes
  crossed to neutral/slower. Do not infer the same cutoff for insertion copies.

V3. SIMULATED BLUEPRINT + IN-PLACE PERMUTATION FLATTENING (NO FREEZE)
- Header: `include/jessesort/v3_inplace_simulated.h`
- Namespace: `jessesort::simulated_inplace_flatten`
- This is a NON-FREEZING variation. Insertion shares V2 semantics, including E065 routing
  and retained E099 natural-run batching. Flattening converts blueprint tags to destinations and permutes the array in
  place with cycle swaps.
- E109 convergence baseline aligns V3 with V1/V2 on descending-game-first materialization and
  common 3.5/4.25/5.0 density thresholds. V3 still differs architecturally by performing the
  blueprint flattening as an in-place cycle permutation. E111 removes selective PowerSort; E115
  moves V3 onto the same shared ends-based adjacent-pair post-flatten merge driver as V1/V2 while
  retaining adaptive ordered-boundary probing, E045 gallop policy, E085 kernel, and <=96-byte type gate.
- E104 revisited E045 under the current E085/E093/E102 cost model with high trial counts.
  V3 now retains the same final-layout 5/7 gallop-trigger selector used by V2: trigger 5
  only for non-branchless, non-PowerSort layouts with 12-128 runs and largest run <=1.25x
  the mean; trigger 7 remains the default.
- Retained as an experimental reconstruction/memory comparison, not the default V2 path.


V1/V2/V3 NON-FREEZING CONSOLIDATION — RESOLVED THROUGH E115
- V1, V2, and V3 represent the same intended non-freezing patience-pile decomposition; their primary
  architectural differences are physical versus simulated insertion storage and flattening mechanics.
- E109-E114 systematically re-tested historical policy drift across all three variations rather than
  preserving variation-specific assumptions. The retained common policy uses descending-first
  materialization, `earlyRandomLike`, common 3.5/4.25/5.0 density thresholds, adaptive ordered-boundary
  probing, and the retained E045/E085 merge routing/kernels.
- E111 removed selective PowerSort from V1/V2/V3.
- E115 consolidated all three onto the same shared ends-based adjacent-pair post-flatten merge driver.
  After flattening, do not assume V1/V2/V3 need separate merge-driver logic unless a genuinely new
  architecture-specific mechanism provides canonical full-suite evidence.
- The E109-E115 reconciliation series is CLOSED. Do not reopen insertion-shape parity, old PowerSort
  availability, historical boundary-probing/classifier/density exceptions, merge-driver parity, or tag
  numbering merely because older historical records mention them. A new attempt requires a materially
  new mechanism plus the mandatory full V1-V7 canonical acceptance benchmark.

V4. EARLY FREEZE + ONE LARGE DEFERRED OVERFLOW RUN
- Header: `include/jessesort/v4_single_overflow.h`
- Namespace: `jessesort::simulated_early_freeze_single_overflow`
- E049 one-time first-64 hinted/no-hint routing remains outside the long insertion loop.
- Freeze policy: 50% is the decision point. V4 may extend ordinary pile creation to a
  strict next power-of-two cap only under its conservative selected policy; therefore
  50% is not always the exact physical freeze boundary.
- E099 natural-run batching is retained in a freeze-aware form that preserves the freeze
  transition and keeps the power-of-two extension interval scalar.
- Overflow: one contiguous deferred region sorted once with std::sort.
- Merge routing retains the post-insertion Random signal, E085 bidirectional branchless
  kernel, and E093 <=96-byte trivially-copyable merge cutoff. Selected PowerSort-style
  scheduling remains limited to its retained run-structure conditions.

V5. EARLY FREEZE + DEFERRED-SORT 32-ELEMENT OVERFLOW BANDS
- Header: `include/jessesort/v5_deferred_bands.h`
- Namespace: `jessesort::simulated_early_freeze`
- E049 one-time first-64 hinted/no-hint routing; 50% nominal freeze decision point with
  the retained broad selected power-of-two extension policy.
- E099 natural-run batching is retained with the STRONGER V5/V6 gate: after the >=12.5%
  initial monotone prefix, the first continuation run must itself be >=8 before the route
  is enabled. This preserves OrganPipe gains while avoiding the broad-gate Rotated loss.
- Overflow: deferred 32-element bands. E090 uses a fixed specialized network for exact
  full 32-element bands; partial/conservative cases retain std::sort. The network was
  ~28.4% faster locally over 4,096,000 int-band sorts, with diluted whole-sort impact.
- Merge routing retains E085 bidirectional branchless merging and E093 <=96-byte
  trivially-copyable cutoff. V5 keeps its variation-specific dominant-endpoint merge
  deferral; global PowerSort and simple adaptive band shaping were rejected.

V6. EARLY FREEZE + LIVE-SORTED 32-ELEMENT OVERFLOW BANDS
- Header: `include/jessesort/v6_live_bands.h`
- Namespace: `jessesort::simulated_early_freeze_live`
- Shares V5's early-freeze base structure and nominal 50%/selected power-of-two extension.
- Uses the same stricter E099 first-continuation-run >=8 natural-run gate as V5.
- During reconstruction, overflow values are insertion-sorted immediately into their current
  32-element band; unlike V5, the completed band is not gathered unsorted and sorted afterward.
- Merge routing retains E085 bidirectional branchless merging and E093 <=96-byte cutoff.
  Random-like inputs use branchless adjacent merging; non-random inputs preserve the
  retained TimSort-style/adjacent selector. Band size 32 remains current.

V7. AVX2 EXPERIMENTAL SIMULATED VARIATION (V2-DERIVED; NO GENERAL FREEZE)
- Header: `include/jessesort/v7_avx2.h`
- Namespace: `jessesort::simulated_simd_v7`
- V2-derived pipeline with a SIMD-oriented capped-probe front end. Default E076 behavior
  caps each game at eight piles during the first 128 values; excess would-be piles spill
  to a temporary probe-overflow run that is sorted and married into an existing pile.
- Exact-eight int32 pile-tail lookup uses one AVX2 compare. E105 adds a narrow repeated
  hint-centered one-register AVX2 continuation for 5k-20k, low-pile layouts (<=12 ordinary
  post-probe piles); broader/global and large-n windowing were rejected. Longer one-shot
  probes and sequential multi-register AVX2 scans remain closed.
- E099 natural-run batching is retained without changing capped-probe behavior on Random.
- E095 fixed the old public-path discrepancy: normalized reconstruction is now the default
  capped-probe reconstruction. E096 then removed its separate `step[]` stream entirely;
  reconstruction improved modestly while whole-sort effect was near neutral.
- E089 retained bidirectional branchless merging and E093 retained the <=96-byte
  trivially-copyable merge cutoff.
- V7+ numbering remains reserved for SIMD-oriented experiments.

E109 COMMON NON-FREEZING BASELINE — CURRENT POLICY
- E109 intentionally stopped treating historical V1/V2/V3 policy differences as prerequisites
  for further research. Instead it cherry-picked the strongest/recently retained shared policies
  into one common starting point, while preserving the core representation difference of each
  variation.
- Common V1/V2/V3 policies after E109:
    * first-64 `earlyRandomLike` prerequisite for Random branchless merge routing;
    * size-aware density thresholds 3.5 / 4.25 / 5.0 (<=20k / mid / >=500k);
    * descending-game runs materialized before ascending-game runs;
    * E045 final-layout 5/7 gallop selector;
    * E085 bidirectional branchless merge and E093 <=96-byte type gate;
    * adaptive ordered-boundary probing;
    * selective PowerSort-style scheduling when `shouldUsePowerSortMerge()` qualifies.
- V1 remains physical-pile insertion with its own merge driver; V2 remains normalized auxiliary
  reconstruction; V3 remains in-place cycle flattening. E109 copies policies, not entire pipelines.
- Simulated tag encoding remains ascending=0 / descending=1 for now. E109 aligns materialized
  order only; renumbering tags would affect the wider simulated family and is not needed for the
  common V1/V2/V3 baseline.
- E109 is deliberately a CONVERGENCE baseline rather than a claim that every transferred policy
  is locally optimal in every variation. Before/after regressions are recorded below and are now
  explicit follow-up targets.

1.3 CURRENT SHARED ENGINEERING DECISIONS
- Numeric sentinels are removed from maintained implementations.
- Copyable, movable, non-default-constructible values are supported. Move-only values
  remain unsupported because pile-tail mirrors are copied.
- Comparators must provide a strict weak ordering. Inputs containing NaNs require an
  explicit total-order comparator such as NaN-last.
- Equality preserves the current game in maintained pile-routing logic.
- Branchless high-entropy two-run merging is bidirectional across V1-V7 (E085-E089).
- The merge-only fast-path size gate is <=96 bytes for trivially copyable values across
  V1-V7 (E092/E093); this is not a universal element-size policy.
- Natural-run batching is retained across V1-V7 (E098/E099), with stricter activation in
  V5/V6 as described above.
- V4-V6 use 50% as the nominal freeze decision point, with retained power-of-two
  extension policies able to move the actual freeze boundary later.
- Conventional even-sample median retained.
- Random%100 samples uniformly from [0,99]. Sorted+Noise uses 5% independent random
  replacements. Misleading jitter workload removed. BlockSorted handles a partial final
  block safely.

1.4 CURRENT BENCHMARK ARTIFACT POLICY
- `benchmarks/benchmark.cpp` is the canonical whole-sort benchmark.
- Each invocation writes an isolated, gitignored `results/run_*` directory containing
  `benchmark_results.md`, `benchmark_results.csv`, `benchmark_trials.csv`,
  `benchmark_metadata.txt`, and `benchmark_progress.txt`.
- Interrupted runs may be resumed explicitly using the existing run directory; completed
  paired trials are reconstructed from raw CSV and are not rerun.
- Performance decisions use same-run std::sort measurements and paired raw observations.
  Historical CSV collections were removed from the compact repo; decision-relevant
  conclusions remain in this log.

NON-REOPEN RULE
---------------
Do not spend new experiments re-validating the closed E109-E115 reconciliation topics without a
materially new mechanism: insertion-shape parity, selective PowerSort for V1/V2/V3, old ordered-
boundary policies, old random-route/game-balance classifiers, old non-freezing density coefficients,
post-flatten merge-driver parity, or tag/game renumbering. Likewise, do not add a special scheduler
merely because a workload happens to produce very few piles. These paths are not active roadmap items.

For every actual code/performance change below, follow Section 1.1: run the complete canonical
V1-V7 benchmark, resume the same persisted run after timeouts, and display/preserve all seven ratio
tables before retaining the change.

1. FIRST-MERGE-PASS FUSION / RECONSTRUCTION REDESIGN
   - Investigate whether reconstruction can eliminate or fold the first adjacent merge level without
     adding equivalent cross-run comparison/scatter work elsewhere.
   - A viable design should reduce memory traffic/merge levels, not merely move them.

2. V5 DYNAMIC OVERFLOW / MERGE-TREE BALANCING — LOWER PRIORITY / NEEDS A NEW IDEA
   - E059 characterized fixed sizes; E060 rejected simple adaptive/local equalization; E062 rejected
     power-of-two run-count shaping + global PowerSort; E106 rejected data-aware first-boundary shaping.
   - Future work must introduce a materially new mechanism, not retry those policies unchanged.

3. CROSS-PHASE / FREEZE ENGINEERING
   - Re-profile 1m phase costs before reopening freeze thresholds or overflow policies.
   - Shape final runs during insertion only when measured downstream merge savings repay added work.
   - E097 rejected mutable Eytzinger frozen-tail layout decisively; do not retry it unchanged.
   - Any new freeze experiment uses the current 50% nominal decision point and retained extension
     policies as its baseline, not the historical 36.8% baseline.

4. WHOLE-ARRAY SMALLSORT / TINY-N LIBRARY PATH
   - E090 proved a specialized 32-element network can be valuable locally in V5 bands.
   - Separately test a whole-array <=16/32 base case for library-quality tiny sorts.
   - Lower priority for current 100k/1m headline work, but relevant to a general API.

5. NON-POD / EXPENSIVE-COMPARATOR TYPE-AWARE ROUTING
   - E092/E093 established <=96 bytes only for trivially copyable merge values.
   - Test std::string/non-trivial movable records, large records, and deliberately expensive
     comparators separately. Derive thresholds from measured movement/comparison cost.

6. CODE CONSOLIDATION / MODULARIZATION — E064 PLAN, DEFERRED UNTIL ALGORITHM STABILIZES
   - V7 remains close to V2 and should eventually become a thin compile-time search-policy
     specialization.
   - Extract in stages: core tags/result metadata; scalar/SIMD search policies; shared simulated
     insertion; reconstruction/run-layout helpers; merge kernels/routing metadata; then thin wrappers.
   - Avoid virtual/std::function/per-element runtime policy branches. Canonical-benchmark each stage.

7. MAINTENANCE / RELEASE
   - Move-only support design, stability documentation, optional projection API, explicit large-record
     policies, visualization synchronization, GCC/Clang CI, ASan/UBSan, namespace/header cleanup,
     release notes/versioning, packaging or single-header evaluation.

E003 — STATIC EARLY-FREEZE POLICY
Status: historical static baseline; superseded in production V5-V6 by E027 selected power-of-two extension.

History:
- Original uploaded C++ used ceil(1.213*sqrt(n)) as a per-game pile cap; this
  did not match the README/visualization.
- 36.8% global position freeze correctly implemented the documented policy but
  created much more overflow and slowed random input.
- 25% was generally worse and rejected.
- 50% improved routine-size random performance relative to 36.8% and became
  the static baseline used for subsequent early-freeze audits.
- 10%-30% k-versus-m sweeps showed that balancing normal piles and accepted
  post-freeze elements does not necessarily minimize runtime because overflow
  structure and merge cost dominate.

Decision:
- Preserve 50% as the decision point and as the `fixed_half` compatibility/test
  policy. Production V5-V6 now apply the variant-specific selected power-of-two
  extension retained in E027. Larger-n phase instrumentation remains pending.

E027 — POWER-OF-TWO FREEZE TARGETING
Status: retained variant-specifically for V5, V4, and V6.

Question:
- Can early-freeze pile creation stop at a useful power-of-two boundary so the
  reconstructed run set is cheaper to merge, without creating enough extra
  overflow work to erase the benefit?

Rejected interpretations:
- Rounding the 50%-point normal pile count down to the nearest power of two was
  rejected. It froze too early, generated substantially more overflow, and often
  left merge depth unchanged or worse.
- Oracle total-run targeting was used as a structural upper-bound experiment.
  Even when cap selection was free, reducing nominal merge depth alone was not
  sufficient: extra overflow work could dominate. This oracle timing was not
  used as production decision evidence because early prototypes did not preserve
  every production fast path.

Retained interpretation:
- Keep 50% as a decision point rather than an unconditional freeze.
- Measure the combined normal pile count p at that point. If a variant-specific
  selector activates, continue ordinary (unfrozen) pile creation until the
  combined pile count reaches the strict next power of two, 2^k > p, or the
  input ends. Only then enter the normal frozen overflow phase.
- V5/V6 broad selector:
    * always extend when p <= 32;
    * otherwise require n >= 32,768 and either p <= 4*sqrt(n), or the strict
      next power-of-two cap is no more than 1.25*p.
- V4 conservative selector:
    * extend only when p == 1 or 4 <= p <= 32;
    * keep the 50% freeze for p == 2, p == 3, and larger pile sets.
- The direct simulation helper retains `FreezePolicy::fixed_half` as its default
  for compatibility and controlled tests; production V5-V6 opt into their
  selected policies explicitly.

Direct 100k A/B evidence:
- Exact production-like candidate versus untouched fixed-half baseline, identical
  inputs, interleaved order, 40 trials per process, three independent processes.
- Median candidate deltas:

                    V5 deferred32   V4 single run   V6 live8
  Random               -4.02%          +0.22%         -4.36%
  Noise 5%             -0.75%          -2.17%         -2.80%
  Random%100           +2.99%          +1.63%         +4.09%
  Alternating          +0.65%          -1.70%         -0.47%
  Sawtooth             -6.39%         -32.44%         -3.62%
  BlockSorted          -7.15%         -24.24%         -2.89%
  OrganPipe           -34.37%         -29.95%        -50.78%
  Rotated              -1.79%          -4.91%         +2.63%

Scale checks:
- At 10k, the retained policies preserved the structured gains; V5/V6 Random%100
  did not regress materially in that run.
- At 1M with a lower-trial harness, the structured gains persisted, including
  Sawtooth, BlockSorted, and OrganPipe. V5/V6 Random%100 regressed about 5.8%
  and 7.0%, respectively. This duplicate-heavy tradeoff is recorded explicitly
  rather than hidden; the general-input gains were judged more important.

Structural examples at 100k:
- V5 Random: 607 -> 866 normal piles, overflow 16,389 -> 0, total runs
  1,120 -> 866, nominal depth 11 -> 10.
- V5 Sawtooth: 11 -> 16 piles, overflow 49,890 -> 24,920, runs 1,571 -> 795,
  depth 11 -> 10.
- V5 BlockSorted: 9 -> 12 piles, overflow 10,002 -> 0, runs 322 -> 12,
  depth 9 -> 4.
- OrganPipe: all three variants move from one normal pile plus 49,999 overflow
  values to two normal piles and zero overflow. V5 runs fall 1,564 -> 2 and V6
  runs 6,251 -> 2.
- V4 Sawtooth improves about 32% even though its run count rises 12 -> 17 and
  nominal depth rises 4 -> 5. V4 OrganPipe improves about 30% while nominal
  depth remains 1. These cases prove that overflow sorting cost can matter more
  than simply minimizing merge-pass count.

Decision:
- Retain the broad selected power-of-two extension in V5 and V6.
- Retain the conservative low-pile extension in V4.
- Do not use nearest-below power-of-two pile caps.
- Do not optimize only for nominal merge depth; future freeze work must measure
  overflow volume/cost and actual merge traffic together.
- Keep the Random%100 regression visible as a known duplicate-heavy tradeoff.

Validation:

- Targeted Clang ASan/UBSan runs covered early-freeze boundary, single-overflow,

- A post-change controlled six-variant benchmark was saved as the new

E047 — V1 STRUCTURED-SIDE EARLY HINT ROUTING — RETAINED
===============================================================================

Goal
----
Complete the opposite side of E046's early insertion router: E046 learned when
V1 should skip locality assumptions and use direct bit-walk; E047 asks when the
first 64 elements provide enough measured locality to introduce a last-pile hint
that V1 historically did not have.

Probe characterization
----------------------
Across 2,000 seeds at n=100k, exact previous-pile hit rates in the first 64 were
~24.4% Random, ~25.2% Random%100, ~90.5% Sorted+Noise(5%), and essentially 100%
for the strongly structured routine families. Random/Random%100 were within +/-1
pile only ~63%, while Sorted+Noise was ~99.2%. However, Alternating also had 100%
exact locality with only one pile per game, demonstrating that hit rate alone is
not enough: direct search of a 1-pile set is cheaper than hint validation.

Retained route
--------------
For elements 65..n, after the exact first-64 probe:
  * E046 Random-like (>=6 piles in each game) -> direct bit-walk, unchanged.
  * Otherwise, if there are >=16 hint observations, >=4 total piles, and exact
    previous-pile hit rate >=75% -> validate the per-game last-pile hint first;
    on miss, immediately use V1's ordinary binary pile-tail search.
  * Otherwise -> existing ordinary binary pile-tail search.
No unbounded adjacent walking is introduced. Merge routing remains independent
and final-layout based.

------------
100k, 300 trials seed 9100000 after the >=4-pile guard:
  Random             4767.26 -> 4769.90 us   ~0.06% slower
  Sorted+Noise(5%)   1735.15 -> 1488.13 us   14.24% faster
  Random%100         1605.96 -> 1608.78 us   ~0.18% slower
  Alternating         493.36 ->  493.33 us   neutral
  all other routine families approximately neutral.

100k, 250 trials seed 19100000:
  Sorted+Noise(5%)   1733.78 -> 1486.96 us   14.24% faster
  Random/Random%100/Alternating approximately neutral.

10k, 300 trials:
  Sorted+Noise(5%)    139.226 -> 126.687 us   9.01% faster.

E053 — SIZE-AWARE FINAL PILE-DENSITY ROUTING
Date: 2026-08-08
Status: RETAINED
===============================================================================

Question:
- Is E052's normalized branchless-merge cutoff (final_piles^2 >= 7*n) stable
  across 10k, 100k, and 1M, or does the merge-kernel crossover itself move
  with problem size?

Forced-kernel scale audit:
- The crossover is NOT scale-invariant. Approximate observed normalized final
  pile densities and branchless/general behavior were:
    * n=10k: density ~5.9 was near parity; >=6.5 clearly favored branchless.
      Full Random density ~7.15 and branchless was ~11% faster.
    * n=100k: crossover occurred around density ~6.5-6.8. Full Random density
      ~7.61 and branchless was ~14% faster.
    * n=1M: density ~7.11 (Random-K=10k) still slightly favored general, while
      full Random density ~7.85 favored branchless by ~16%.
- Therefore one universal coefficient necessarily misroutes at least one size
  regime: 7*n is too conservative at 10k and too permissive for some 1M
  duplicate-heavy layouts.

Retained size-aware rule:
- For n <= 20k: branchless if final_piles^2 >= 6*n.
- For 20k < n < 500k: branchless if 4*final_piles^2 >= 27*n (~6.75*n).
- For n >= 500k: branchless if 2*final_piles^2 >= 15*n (~7.5*n).
- The first-64 earlyRandomLike requirement and eligible-type guards remain
  unchanged; this only refines the independent final-layout decision.

Direct size-aware versus universal-7 A/B:
- n=10k: large wins where universal 7 incorrectly stayed general, commonly
  ~5-8% and reaching ~15% in tested Random-K regimes; full Random was ~1%
  faster/near neutral because both policies are near the high-density end.
- n=100k: essentially neutral overall around the already-calibrated E052
  regime; targeted medium-cardinality cases showed small wins with no broad
  regression.
- n=1M: Random-K=10k improved ~6% because the size-aware rule correctly kept
  the general/galloping kernel; full Random remained on branchless and was
  effectively neutral apart from process noise.

Validation:

Interpretation / next router work:
- Final pile density is useful, but its optimal cutoff is size-dependent. This
  strengthens the two-stage design: early structure identifies insertion

- Continue the multi-regime router by testing a cheap duplicate/saturation
  signal for INSERTION decisions; first-64 pile count alone cannot separate

E055 — V2 NATURAL-RUN SCANNING / PIECEWISE-MONOTONE BATCHING PROTOTYPE
Date: 2026-08-08
===============================================================================

Goal
----
Test active Item 6: use source monotone runs to avoid repeated pile searches and
shape simulated piles more cheaply on structured inputs, while protecting Random.

Opportunity profile
-------------------
At n=100k over 30 seeds, profiling strict source runs >=4 elements found that
compatible whole-run-to-one-pile assignments are essentially nonexistent for
Random and Random%100, but materially present on structured inputs. Among
eligible run elements, compatibility was about 47.6% for Sorted+Noise(5%),
39.8% for BlockSorted, ~4.9% for Sawtooth, and ~100% for OrganPipe/Rotated.

Prototype
---------
For a detected strict monotone run, compare the first and terminal element's
destination against the current tail array. If both resolve to the same pile,
assign the entire run's blueprint tags at once, add the run length to that pile's
count, and update its tail to the terminal value. This preserves the simulated
representation without materializing physical piles. Incompatible runs fall back
to ordinary per-element insertion.

Result
------
A broad structured scanner was rejected. Sawtooth and some semi-structured cases
contain long source runs whose values span multiple patience piles, so scanning
them adds work without reducing searches. The promising sub-regime is strongly
piecewise-monotone input: prototypes repeatedly improved OrganPipe by roughly
16-19% and Rotated by roughly 21-28% at 100k. BlockSorted could also improve
substantially (~7-16%) with permissive scanning, but the gain was not robust once
miss-abandon guards were tightened. Random/Random%100 showed no useful long-run
opportunity.

Decision
--------
NO PRODUCTION CHANGE. Preserve the idea as a narrower piecewise-monotone router
branch. The next materially different test should classify a long compatible
continuation cheaply (for example, long initial prefix + next-run boundary
compatibility) before entering the run scanner, rather than scanning all
structured runs.

E056 - V1 STRUCTURED HINT MISS REFINEMENT AUDIT
Date: 2026-08-08
Status: rejected; no production sorting change.

Question:
- After E047's retained high-locality exact previous-pile hint, can a hint miss
  still be exploited cheaply by checking an adjacent pile, restricting the
  search direction, or changing the miss fallback from binary search to
  bit-walk?

Method:
- V1 only, 100k canonical/int workloads, same-binary runtime A/B.
- Retained E047 activation rule was preserved: non-Random-like first-64 prefix,
  >=4 total probe piles, >=16 usable hint observations, >=75% exact hint hits.
- Compared:
    A. retained exact-hint -> ordinary binary search on miss;
    B. one adjacent-pile check in the indicated direction -> bit-walk on miss;
    C. failed-hint direction -> binary search restricted to the correct half;
    D. exact-hint -> bit-walk on miss (control);
    E. adjacent check -> ordinary binary search on miss (control).
- Two independent seeds, 100 trials per family for the main comparison.
- Follow-up noise-prefix profiling used 5000 prefixes per noise level.

Main findings:
- The adjacent candidate initially appeared promising on Sorted+Noise(5%):
  ~5.1% and ~4.6% faster on the first two seeds.
- Control separation showed the adjacent check itself was not responsible:
  adjacent->binary was neutral, while exact-hint->bit-walk showed an even larger
  apparent gain (~6.7%) in the multi-mode experimental build.
- A clean outer dispatch was then tested so the >=85% locality decision was paid
  once after the probe rather than inside each miss. Under that cleaner layout,
  exact-hint->bit-walk was neutral-to-slower across the 0.5%-30% noise sweep;
  no robust whole-sort gain remained.
- Direction-restricted binary search also failed to produce a repeatable gain.
- Probe locality remains informative: mean exact hit rate was ~90.5% at 5%
  noise, ~85.1% at 7.5%, ~79.8% at 10%, with the existing >=75% route naturally
  deactivating as disorder rises. But no tested miss kernel improves on the
  retained ordinary binary fallback robustly enough to justify more routing.

Decision:
- Keep E047's exact previous-pile hint with ordinary binary-search fallback.
- Reject one-adjacent-pile and direction-restricted fallback kernels in their
  tested forms.
- Do not retain exact-hint->bit-walk fallback; its apparent gain was sensitive
  to experimental code layout and disappeared under a clean outer dispatch.
- Future V1 insertion work should move to materially different search kernels
  (especially SIMD/small-pile lookup) rather than further scalar miss-routing
  permutations.


E057 - V7 FIRST SIMD VARIATION: AVX2 EXACT-EIGHT PILE SEARCH
Date: 2026-08-08

Policy decision:
- SIMD work now receives separate variation numbers V7+ so scalar/general
  V1-V6 remain clean comparison baselines.

Experiment:
- AVX2 int32 pile-tail lookup broadcasts the inserted value, compares against
  eight tails in one 256-bit register, converts the comparison to an 8-bit lane
  mask, and uses ctz to locate the insertion boundary. Generic types and custom
  comparators use the ordinary scalar search.
- Isolated lookup sweep compared scalar bit-walk to sequential AVX2 scans at
  pile counts 4..256. Exactly eight tails was the only clear SIMD win: about
  0.75-0.77x scalar lookup time (~23-25% faster). Four tails was slower, and
  12+ tails was substantially slower because scanning multiple vectors loses to
  logarithmic scalar search.
- A scalar-search-block + one-AVX-vector hybrid was also tested and rejected;
  its scalar block search/setup overhead made it slower for 12+ tails.
- Therefore V7 deliberately enables SIMD only when tails.size()==8.

Whole-sort causal A/B:
- To avoid attributing copied-header/code-placement effects to SIMD, V7 was
  compared against a layout-matched scalar clone, two independent seeds, 80
  trials, canonical generators. At 100k:
    * Random: V7/scalar 0.9949 and 0.9912 (~0.5-0.9% faster).
    * Random%100: 0.9918 and 0.9922 (~0.8% faster).
    * Other structured cases were mostly small/noisy; no broad structured claim
      is attributed to the exact-eight SIMD operation.
- Direct V2-vs-V7 timings show larger structured differences in some workloads,
  but those cannot be causally attributed to an operation that fires only at
  exactly eight tails; treat them as code-layout differences, not SIMD evidence.

Validation:
- 5000 randomized ascending + descending-comparator fuzz cases passed for V7.
- Full maintained make test suite passed.

Next:
- Before AVX-512, test probe-gated AVX2 small-pile continuation (E058).
- Also test non-linear SIMD search structures if proposed; do not extend V7 to
  sequential multi-register scans, which E057 rejected.

E058 - PROBE-GATED AVX2 SMALL-PILE CONTINUATION
Date: 2026-08-08
Status: rejected; V7 unchanged; no new production variation retained.

Question:
- Should AVX2 pile-tail lookup be treated primarily as an early/small-pile
  insertion optimization, with the first-64 probe deciding whether SIMD remains
  active after the probe, rather than allowing the V7 exact-eight specialization
  to fire anywhere in the full insertion?

Opportunity profile:
- At 100k, counting moments where the active game had exactly eight tails showed
  that full Random had only ~5 opportunities before element 64 and ~8 after it,
  while Random%100 had ~7 before / ~10 after. By contrast, structured low-pile
  cases can revisit or remain at eight tails much longer: Sawtooth ~4999
  post-probe opportunities and BlockSorted ~3750 in the simple pile-count
  profile. This supports using the probe to distinguish sustained-small-pile
  structure from high-growth Random before considering a continuation route.

Experiment:
- Built an experimental V2/V7-derived implementation in which AVX2 remained
  available during the first 64 processed values, then a single outer dispatch
  selected a SIMD-capable or scalar continuation from the probe pile count.
- Tested total-probe-pile ceilings 4, 8, 12, and 16. The active SIMD continuation
  still used AVX2 only at exactly eight tails; it never sequentially scanned
  multiple vectors.
- Initial separate-header comparisons again showed large structured movements
  caused by code layout, so those results were not treated as SIMD evidence.
- Repeated the test with a runtime SIMD on/off control inside the exact same
  compiled implementation so both sides shared code layout. Three independent
  seeds were used for the main 100k causal checks where completed.

Causal result:
- Probe-gated post-64 SIMD did not produce a stable advantage. With an 8-pile
  probe ceiling, mean SIMD/scalar ratios were approximately:
    Random 0.996, Random%100 0.999, Sorted+Noise5 0.996,
    Sawtooth 1.000, BlockSorted 1.000, OrganPipe 1.002,
    Rotated 1.005 (individual seeds fluctuated around parity).
- Changing the ceiling to 4 or 12 did not reveal a robust continuation regime;
  apparent structured gains in copied-header builds disappeared or became noisy
  under the same-code runtime control.
- A final probe-only isolation (SIMD permitted during the first 64 values and
  unconditionally disabled afterward) averaged roughly:
    Random 0.9956 (~0.4% faster), Random%100 0.9988,
    Sorted+Noise5 0.9955, Sawtooth 0.9996, BlockSorted 0.9988,
    OrganPipe 1.0038, Rotated 0.9981.
  The Random result was not stable enough across seeds (one ~1.4% win, other
  seeds near parity/slightly slower) to justify a production route.

Decision:
- Do NOT add a probe-gated AVX2 continuation variation.
- Keep V7 as the existing exact-eight experimental proof-of-concept.
- Treat E057's larger separate-header whole-sort movements cautiously: E058's
  same-code runtime control shows that the causal effect of exact-eight SIMD is
  very small at whole-sort scale.
- The user's architectural expectation is confirmed: once pile growth expands,
  AVX2 linear tail lookup should not be routed as a general post-probe search.
- The next SIMD family may test AVX-512 exact-sixteen lookup as its own V8, or a
  materially different SIMD search structure; do not revisit sequential AVX2
  multi-register scanning without a new algorithmic idea.

E060 - V5 FIRST ADAPTIVE/NONUNIFORM RUN-SHAPING PASS
Date: 2026-08-08
Status: SIMPLE HEURISTICS REJECTED; TREE-AWARE BALANCING REMAINS OPEN

Goal
----
Move beyond E059's fixed band-size sweep and test whether cheap post-insertion
metadata can shape V5 overflow runs better than the fixed-32 baseline, including
a first bounded live pile-pair balancing experiment.

Method
------
- Canonical V5 insertion/freeze and final merge scheduler held fixed.
- n=100k, canonical generators, two independent seeds, 40 trials/seed.
- Same-binary rotated execution order; every output checked against std::sort.
- Adaptive overflow layouts tested:
    * by_run_count: nearest-power-of-two band size from overflow/frozen-run count,
      clamped to 16..1024;
    * by_median: nearest-power-of-two band size from median frozen-run length;
    * boundary_then32: if first overflow run pairs with the last frozen run,
      match that boundary run (bounded 16..1024), then return to 32;
    * boundary_then_adaptive: boundary match followed by adaptive run-count size.
- Separate live-balancing control: when the final two frozen runs pair on the
  first merge pass and the last run is smaller by <= 8/16/32 values, sort that
  many leading overflow values and merge them into the last frozen run before
  constructing the remaining fixed-32 overflow bands.

Adaptive sizing results
-----------------------
- Global metadata-derived sizing is not a general win. At 100k, run-count and
  median-derived sizes were roughly neutral on Random/Random%100 but regressed:
    Sawtooth ~10-11%, BlockSorted ~6-11%, Rotated ~8-14% across the two seeds.
- boundary_then32 was consistently far safer: generally within ~0.5% of fixed32,
  with occasional small structured wins (one Rotated seed ~2.6%). This indicates
  that the useful degrees of freedom are local merge boundaries, not a global
  replacement for 32.
- boundary_then_adaptive inherited the global-size regressions and is rejected.

Bounded last-pair top-up results
--------------------------------
- The simple "equalize a close pair" rule did not generalize.
- A 32-value cap occasionally helped (seed1 Sorted+Noise ~3.6%, Random%100 ~1.7%),
  but those gains did not repeat on seed2 and BlockSorted/OrganPipe could regress.
- 8/16-value caps likewise fluctuated around parity with workload-dependent wins
  and losses.
- Therefore local equality alone is not a sufficient objective. Equalizing two
  run lengths can add work without reducing merge depth or run count.

Decision
--------
- No V5 production change.
- Reject simple global adaptive band sizing and blind bounded pair equalization.
- KEEP the larger V5 idea open: overflow is a dynamic merge-tree balancing
  resource, but the router/planner must explicitly estimate the projected merge
  tree and compare saved merge depth/moves against the cost of sorting/inserting
  the consumed overflow fragment.
- Next V5 shaping attempt should simulate the actual adjacent merge schedule from
  run lengths and select overflow boundaries to reduce predicted total merge
  traffic/depth, rather than optimizing local run-size symmetry.

E062 — V5 projected merge-tree shaping: power-of-two run counts + scheduler audit
Status: completed; no production code change retained.

Goal:
- Continue E060's V5 dynamic merge-tree balancing track with a planner that scores
  the actual downstream merge structure rather than merely equalizing neighboring
  run sizes.

Experiment A — power-of-two total-run planner:
- Baseline remained canonical V5 fixed-32 deferred overflow bands.
- Candidate repartitioned the same overflow so frozen_runs + overflow_runs fell to
  the largest feasible power-of-two run-count boundary below the fixed-32 baseline.
- Tested unconstrained and with 64/128/256 maximum deferred-band sizes.
- 100k, two seeds, canonical generators.

Result:
- No broad gain. Full Random and Random%100 were approximately neutral.
- Sorted+Noise and Sawtooth were typically ~0.5-1.8% slower.
- BlockSorted was ~1-3% slower.
- Rotated was unstable across seeds (one apparent win, one large loss).
- Rejected. The current adjacent-round merge plus ordered-boundary elimination does
  not behave like a simple textbook balanced tree where hitting a power-of-two run
  count automatically removes enough work to pay for larger deferred sorts.

Structural conclusion:
- Under the current adjacent-pair scheduler, changing sibling run sizes alone has
  limited leverage because most elements still participate once per merge level.
  Overflow shaping becomes substantially more meaningful only when it either
  changes run count/levels, enables boundary elimination, or is paired with a
  scheduler that actually uses run sizes.

Experiment B — scheduler audit on fixed-32 V5 runs:
- Compared V5's current defer_dominant_first_endpoint adjacent scheduler against
  generic selective PowerSort and always-PowerSort, with reconstruction/bands held
  fixed at 32.
- 100k, two seeds, canonical generators.

Representative ratios to current V5:
- Random: selective ~1.000; always PowerSort ~1.15 (large regression).
- Sorted+Noise(5%): selective/always ~1.08 (large regression).
- Random%100: approximately neutral to ~2% slower.
- Sawtooth: PowerSort ~1.7-3.1% faster.
- BlockSorted: ~0-2% faster depending seed.
- OrganPipe: approximately neutral.
- Rotated: highly seed/layout sensitive; one seed near neutral, one ~4-5% faster.

Decision:
- Do NOT globally couple V5 to PowerSort and do NOT retain the power-of-two run-count
  planner.
- Keep the broader V5 idea open: deferred overflow remains a dynamic merge-tree
  balancing resource, but future planners must route BOTH overflow partitioning and
  merge scheduler from actual post-insertion structure. A useful candidate must
  predict when a size-aware scheduler wins and then shape runs specifically for that
  scheduler; fixed adjacent merging cannot fully exploit arbitrary run-size shaping.

Goal:

Key findings:
- V7 is effectively a fork of V2: simulated_simd_v7.hpp is 1,638 lines versus

1. shared core types/tags/result metadata;

3. V2/V7 ordinary simulated insertion engine parameterized by search policy;

E066 — Benchmark methodology hardening after VM/std::sort drift audit
Status: HISTORICALLY RETAINED, BUT SEED/TRIAL CADENCE SUPERSEDED BY E077; no sorting algorithm changed.
Current-reading note: retain E066's paired/same-process/robust-statistics principles,
but use Section 1.1/E077 for the current trial and seed design.

Why this was necessary:
- Repeated canonical runs showed that absolute std::sort medians can move enough
  between VM/host sessions to overlap the size of current JesseSort optimizations.
- E054 was later falsified by preserved-version recompilation: an apparently
  replicated 3-6% result came from harness/code-layout sensitivity rather than the
  intended mechanism. This demonstrated that 50-100 trial separate-run medians are
  no longer strong enough evidence for small retain/revert decisions.
- Saved absolute std::sort medians are therefore NOT a portable calibration constant.
  They are useful historical evidence only when the execution environment is known
  to be comparable. Same-run paired measurements are the primary evidence.

Canonical benchmark standard from E066 onward:
1. Default benchmark size remains n=100000 unless an experiment targets scale.
2. Default is TWO deterministic seeds (9100000 and 123456789).
3. Default is 500 timed trials PER SEED: 1000 paired observations per workload.
4. Claims below ~1.5% should use >=1000 trials per seed, or a phase-isolated causal
   harness with equal or stronger evidence.
5. Baseline/candidate/std::sort must run in the SAME executable/process whenever
   possible and receive the EXACT SAME source vector for each paired trial.
6. Execution order rotates every trial and reverses on alternating 8-trial blocks,
   distributing warm-cache, boost-clock, and order-position effects across methods.
7. Correctness validation remains OUTSIDE the timed region.
8. Short untimed warmups precede each input family.
9. Do not trim outliers by default. Preserve raw timings and report robust spread:
   median, p25, p75, IQR, and MAD.
10. Report BOTH:
    - ratio of aggregate medians, for continuity with historical tables; and
    - median of per-trial paired ratios, which is preferred for small same-run claims.
11. Preserve per-trial raw data so candidate effects can be reanalyzed without
    rerunning and so order/seed sensitivity can be inspected.
12. Emit per-seed summaries in addition to pooled summaries. A retained small gain
    should not depend on one seed unless the mechanism is explicitly seed/data-shape
    specific.
13. For important micro-optimizations, prefer phase-isolated/layout-matched causal
    harnesses in addition to whole-sort timing.
14. Cross-run absolute timing comparisons are secondary evidence. Preserved-version
    recompilation in one current harness is preferred when auditing regressions.

benchmark.cpp changes:
- Default changed from 30 trials/one implicit seed to 500 trials/seed x 2 seeds.
- Canonical seeds standardized to 9100000 and 123456789.
- Same-input paired trial records added.
- Balanced rotating/reversing execution order added.
- Two untimed warmup trials/input by default.
- benchmark_results.csv changed to long-form robust summaries with median, quartiles,
  IQR, MAD, ratio-of-medians, and paired-ratio quartiles.
- benchmark_trials.csv added with every raw time, std::sort partner time, paired ratio,
  seed, trial number, and execution-order position.
- benchmark_metadata.txt added with trial configuration, compiler, CPU model when
  available, hardware concurrency, AVX2 compile status, timestamp, and a warning that
  absolute medians are not portable across VM sessions.
- Per-seed and pooled summaries are both emitted.

Interpretation policy:
- For a candidate-vs-baseline optimization experiment, the most persuasive number is
  the paired candidate/baseline distribution from a same-binary harness, not two
  independently measured medians from different builds/runs.
- std::sort ratios remain useful for external competitiveness, but they should be
  computed from std::sort timings collected in that SAME benchmark run.
- Historical std::sort ratios should not by themselves trigger a retain/revert decision.

E070 — Isolated/checkpointed benchmark artifacts + native build standard
Status: benchmark infrastructure only; no sorting algorithm changed.

Motivation:
- Long benchmark tool/terminal calls can time out even when the benchmark process has
  started or completed useful work. Results must survive loss of the interactive response.
- Old benchmark artifacts must never be accidentally consumed by a later timing summary.
- Benchmark builds should use the intended local CPU target while comparing JesseSort and
  std::sort in the same binary; the canonical Makefile benchmark build now uses
  `-O3 -march=native -DNDEBUG` instead of globally forcing only `-mavx2`.

Artifact isolation/checkpoint policy:
1. Every benchmark invocation creates a unique `results/run_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS[_N]/` directory.
2. The benchmark NEVER reads prior run directories when computing a current summary.
3. `benchmark_trials.csv` is appended for each completed paired trial and flushed every
   10 trials (and at each seed boundary/end), so raw timing evidence survives ordinary
   terminal/tool response timeouts.
4. `benchmark_results.csv` contains only the current invocation and is flushed after every
   completed workload; a timeout therefore leaves summaries for all completed workloads.
5. `benchmark_progress.txt` is rewritten during the run with the active input, seed, trial,
   and state, allowing partial-run recovery/diagnosis.
6. `benchmark_metadata.txt` is written at startup in the same run directory.
7. `/results/` is gitignored. Generated timing artifacts are intentionally ephemeral and
   should be exported/shared as needed, then removed with `make clean-results`.
8. No top-level packaged benchmark-result snapshot is retained; this avoids stale results
   being mistaken for current evidence.

Makefile targets:
- `make bench200`: 200 trials, one seed, n=100000 (exploratory only).
- `make bench500` / `make run`: canonical 500 trials/seed x 2 seeds, n=100000.
- `make bench1000`: 1000 trials/seed x 2 seeds for small effects.
- `make clean-results`: deletes the entire ignored results tree.

This E070 policy supplements E066; E066 remains the statistical methodology standard.

E074 — V7 capped 64-element SIMD probe with deferred overflow marriage
Status: EXPERIMENTAL / CAP-ONLY RESULT NEUTRAL; default V7 behavior unchanged.

Question:
- Can V7 make its exact-eight AVX2 lookup materially useful by capping each game's
  pile growth during the existing 64-element structure probe, spilling would-be
  new piles to one temporary overflow run, and restoring patience-tail ordering
  immediately after the routing decision?

Implementation tested:
- Added an experimental runtime `sortWithProbePileCap(values, cap)` path. Public
  `sort(values)` still passes cap=0 and therefore preserves default V7 behavior.
- During the first 64 input elements only, each ascending/descending game can be
  capped independently at 8 or 16 piles. A value whose search result would create
  cap+1 is tagged as probe overflow instead of extending the tail array.
- Routing is decided BEFORE overflow marriage, using probe run-length concentration
  (ordinary pile lengths plus overflow length) rather than raw pile count.
- The overflow is then insertion-sorted/std::sort-sorted as one ascending run and
  immediately married into the existing ascending pile selected by its final/max
  element. If the max would create cap+1, it marries into the boundary pile while
  preserving that pile's larger tail; this detail is required for valid future
  patience insertion.
- Reconstruction skips overflow tags and merges the sorted overflow values into the
  selected reconstructed pile before the normal final merge phase.
- The experimental 16-wide probe search can use at most two AVX2 registers, but only
  while cap=16 is explicitly selected; normal V7 remains exact-eight only.

Correctness:
- An initial incorrect boundary-tail update was caught immediately at 100k Random.
  It was fixed before timing: the merged pile tail is max(existing tail, overflow max),
  not unconditionally overflow max.
- Cap-8 and cap-16 then passed correctness validation across all ten benchmark families.

Activation profile, 100 trials, n=100k:
- Random cap-8: overflow activated in 61/100 probes; 163 total spill values; max spill 7.
- Random%100 cap-8: 55/100 probes; 126 total spill values; max spill 6.
- Other standard workloads: no cap-8 overflow in this sample.
- Cap-16: 0/100 activations on every standard workload, including Random. The
  per-game tail sets simply do not reach 16 often enough within 64 elements.

Causal timing:
- Cross-build timings were discarded for retain/revert decisions because they again
  showed large code-layout swings on structured inputs.
- A same-binary A/B/C harness compares baseline V7, cap-8, cap-16, and std::sort while
  rotating execution order.
- 500 paired Random trials at n=100k:
    cap-8 / baseline V7  = 0.999508
    cap-16 / baseline V7 = 0.998759 (NOT mechanism evidence; cap-16 never activates)
    baseline V7 / std    = 0.909742
    cap-8 V7 / std       = 0.910209
- Therefore the cap-8-only 64-element probe is effectively neutral. The probe is too
  short for the SIMD lookup savings to amortize overflow bookkeeping/marriage, and
  cap-16 is not exercised at all.

Decision / next step:
- Do NOT change default V7 to probe capping.
- Keep the experimental helper and `v7-probe-cap-experiment` harness for causal work.
- Move the SIMD track to fixed-width insertion CHUNKS (likely 8 AVX2 int32 tails),
  preserving the user's routing-before-marriage rule at chunk/probe boundaries.
- Instrument future chunk experiments with SIMD-hit counts, spill counts/lengths,
  and marriage/reconstruction cost; the goal is to demonstrate useful whole-sort
  SIMD work, not merely a faster isolated eight-tail lookup.

Motivation:
- The prior benchmark grouped hundreds of timing repetitions under only one or two
  base RNG seeds. Paired timing was already correct, but this oversampled a very small
  set of random-input streams and under-sampled input-to-input variability.
- The main benchmark should characterize algorithm performance across many distinct
  deterministic inputs while preserving exact within-trial pairing against std::sort.
- The 1m workload is roughly an order of magnitude more expensive, so the canonical
  sweep uses fewer trials there to keep a full four-size run practical.

New canonical methodology:
- Every measured trial receives its own deterministic RNG seed derived from
  (base seed, input family, n, trial index).
- That exact generated source vector is copied to V1-V7 and std::sort within the trial.
- The reported comparison remains the median of per-trial paired ratios
  algorithm_time / std_sort_time.
- Execution order still rotates/reverses across trials to reduce systematic order bias.
- Structured deterministic inputs naturally repeat when their generator does not use RNG;
  randomized workloads receive a genuinely different deterministic sample each trial.
- Raw CSV records the actual per-trial seed used, making every randomized trial exactly
  reproducible without a separate seed-cohort dimension.

Canonical `make run` / `make run500` / `make bench500` profile:
- n=1k:   500 distinct paired trials
- n=10k:  500 distinct paired trials
- n=100k: 500 distinct paired trials
- n=1m:    50 distinct paired trials
- A targeted single-size invocation uses the requested trial count directly; the 10:1
  1m reduction applies only to the standard all-size sweep.

Reporting/checkpoint changes:
- Markdown and metadata explicitly list the trial count for each size and describe the
  unique deterministic seed policy.
- The obsolete per-seed summary rows were removed; summary rows now represent the full
  independent-trial sample for each input/size.
- Progress checkpointing tracks completed trials against that size's actual trial count.

Interpretation:
- This is a benchmark-quality/reproducibility change only. It does not alter V1-V7 logic.
- For randomized workloads, 500 trials now means 500 distinct generated instances rather
  than repeated measurements organized around only one or two RNG streams.

E085 — V2 BIDIRECTIONAL BRANCHLESS MERGE (RETAIN)
Date: 2026-08-10
Status: RETAINED FOR V2; CROSS-VARIATION PROPAGATION NOT YET ASSUMED

Origin / hypothesis:
- ipnsort's small merge machinery highlighted a limitation in E082's source-level
  four-selection unroll. E082 performs four `takeOne()` calls per loop, but each
  decision advances either left or right, so the next comparison depends on the
  previous result. The unroll reduces loop overhead without creating four independent
  comparison chains.
- Hypothesis: keep the same two-run merge tree but merge from both ends at once. One
  selection chooses the minimum from the two run fronts while an independent selection
  chooses the maximum from the two run backs. This should expose two independent
  compare/select/address chains to out-of-order execution without the extra winner
  selection that made E082's general 4-run fusion ~54-57% slower.

Variation scope:
- V2 only for the retained production change.
- `mergeRunsFromTmpToArr()` gained an optional bidirectional-branchless flag defaulting
  to false, so V3-V6 continue using E082 despite sharing this helper. V1/V7 have their
  own branchless kernels and are unchanged.
- V2 passes true only after its existing E053/E063 Random branchless routing selects the
  branchless two-run kernel. General/galloping/PowerSort paths are unchanged.

Kernel semantics:
- Preserve E082 raw pointers, adjacent two-run scheduling, ordered and reverse-disjoint
  fast paths, buffers, and comparator semantics.
- Maintain source ranges [leftLo,leftHi) and [rightLo,rightHi) plus output bounds
  [outLo,outHi). Each dual-ended iteration performs one forward minimum selection and
  one backward maximum selection.
- Forward ties take left. Backward ties take right because output is written from the
  end; this preserves the old overlapping-merge equal-key order.
- Stop dual-ended processing before either run has fewer than two remaining elements,
  then finish the small/skewed residual middle using the proven forward branchless
  merge logic.

Correctness / safety:
- Temporary exhaustive randomized run-pair harness tested left/right lengths 1..64,
  100 trials per size pair, with keys sampled from [-8,8] to force many duplicates.
  Candidate output exactly matched E082 and was sorted in every test.
- The same harness passed AddressSanitizer + UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer.
- Same-binary full-sort A/B required candidate output to equal both E082 output and
  std::sort on every trial; all passed.
- Normal production benchmark also passed per-trial correctness validation for all
  V1-V7 + std::sort across all ten 100k workloads.

Same-binary Random A/B (temporary harness, alternating/rotating execution order):

    merge candidate/E082 = 0.575016  (~42.50% faster)

- 100k, 500 paired trials:

    full candidate/E082  = 0.672180  (~32.78% faster)
    E082/std median paired ratio = 0.859365
    E085/std median paired ratio = 0.585564
- 1m, 50 paired trials:
    merge candidate/E082 = 0.544313  (~45.57% faster)
    full candidate/E082  = 0.665185  (~33.48% faster)
    E082/std median paired ratio = 0.877348
    E085/std median paired ratio = 0.587552

Comparison-count diagnostic on identical reconstructed Random runs:
- 10k: E082 81,646 comparisons; E085 89,618 (1.09764x, ~9.8% MORE).
- 100k: E082 949,959; E085 991,725 (1.04397x, ~4.4% MORE).

E097 - V5 FROZEN PILE-TAIL EYTZINGER SEARCH (REJECTED)
------------------------------------------------------
Motivation:
  Test whether V5's post-freeze pile-tail search could benefit from Eytzinger/tree
  layout after the pile count becomes fixed. This targeted frozen insertion only;
  freeze policy, overflow bands, reconstruction, E090 smallsort, and merge behavior
  were unchanged. A literal pre-E097 copy of V5 was made before the refactor.

Important structural observation:
  Freeze makes the NUMBER of piles immutable, but not their tail values. Existing
  pile tails continue to be replaced after every successful frozen insertion. The
  candidate therefore used a fixed Eytzinger heap mapping plus logical<->heap maps
  and updated the corresponding heap node whenever a logical pile tail changed.
  It did not assume an invalid immutable-value search structure.

Method:
  V2-style Random/no-hint route only. Same-process alternating A/B on identical
  Random int inputs. Baseline used the retained bit-walk search over monotone tail
  vectors. Candidate built Eytzinger layouts at the actual freeze point and used
  tree traversal for frozen searches while preserving identical blueprint/count/
  overflow semantics. Insertion metadata and final sorted output were required to
  match exactly on every paired trial.

Results (candidate / baseline median paired ratio):
  n=10k, 1000 trials:
    insertion 1.3773  (~37.7% slower)
    full sort 1.1635  (~16.4% slower)
  n=100k, 600 trials:
    insertion 0.9980  (effectively neutral)
    full sort 0.9971  (effectively neutral)
  n=1m, 120 trials:
    insertion 1.7233  (~72.3% slower)
    full sort 1.2353  (~23.5% slower)

Interpretation:
  Eytzinger does not solve a real cache-capacity problem for V5's frozen tails on
  these inputs. Even at n=1m the active tail set is only on the order of thousands
  of int values and is already compact/cache-friendly. The candidate instead adds
  heap-index traversal, logical<->heap mapping, and a second tail update. Unlike
  classic binary search over a large immutable array, V5 continuously mutates the
  searched tails, which further weakens the usual Eytzinger advantage.

Decision:
  REJECT and fully revert V5 to its pre-E097 implementation. Do not retry the same
  Eytzinger representation unchanged. If frozen search is revisited, target fewer
  comparisons/loads or a representation that avoids duplicate tail maintenance.

E098 — V2 NARROW PIECEWISE-MONOTONE NATURAL-RUN ROUTE
Date: 2026-08-10
Status: RETAINED for V2
===============================================================================

Goal
----
Revisit E055 without paying a general source-run scanner on Random/Sawtooth-like
inputs. Use the long monotone prefix already discovered by V2 as a cheap gate, then
skip redundant pile searches only when a later natural run provably maps to one pile.

Design
------
- Gate: the existing strict initial monotone prefix must cover >= 1/8 of n and be at
  least the existing 32-element prefix threshold.
- Once gated, scan later strict monotone source runs. Runs shorter than 8 fall back.
- For a longer run, search only its first and last value against the current tail array.
  If both map to the same pile, fill that run's blueprint tags in bulk, add its length
  to the pile count, and update the pile tail to the terminal value.
- If endpoint destinations differ, process the run element-by-element with the retained
  hinted search. The route changes no merge/reconstruction policy.
- Paired testing required exact equality of blueprint, asc/desc counts, tails, and final
  sorted output between baseline and candidate on every trial.

Evidence
--------
10k, 500 paired trials per family:
- OrganPipe insertion ratio 0.4521; whole sort 0.9390.
- Rotated insertion ratio 0.5337; whole sort 0.9186.
- Random/Sawtooth whole-sort ratios ~1.002; BlockSorted ~1.004.

100k, 300 paired trials per family:
- OrganPipe insertion ratio 0.5090; whole sort 0.8892.
- Rotated insertion ratio 0.5777; whole sort 0.8938.
- Random 0.9918, Sawtooth 0.9908, BlockSorted 0.9849, Sorted+Noise 0.9873
  at whole-sort level; these small apparent gains are not attributed to the gated
  mechanism because those families rarely/never enter it.

1m expanded run, 150 paired trials per family:
- OrganPipe insertion ratio 0.5721 (~42.8% faster); whole sort 0.9169 (~8.3% faster).
- Rotated insertion ratio 0.6618 (~33.8% faster); whole sort 0.9424 (~5.8% faster).
- Random whole sort 1.0128, Sawtooth 1.0039, BlockSorted 1.0033, Sorted+Noise 0.9989:
  effectively neutral guardrails at this trial count.

Interpretation
--------------
E055's mechanism was sound but its activation was too broad. A very long initial
monotone prefix is enough to identify the narrow piecewise-monotone regime cheaply.
For OrganPipe and many Rotated inputs, the remainder contains another long strict run
whose values all belong to one patience pile. Two endpoint searches therefore replace
thousands to hundreds of thousands of redundant per-element pile searches while
preserving exactly the same simulated pile structure.

Decision
--------
RETAIN in V2 with the conservative >=12.5% initial-prefix gate and min run length 8.
Do not broaden the gate yet. Propagation to freeze/overflow variations is a separate
experiment because their post-freeze routing changes the economics of run batching.

E099 — E098 NATURAL-RUN BATCHING PROPAGATION TO V1/V3/V4/V5/V6/V7
Date: 2026-08-10
Status: RETAINED across all six propagation targets, with stricter V5/V6 gate
===============================================================================

Goal
----
Propagate E098's narrow piecewise-monotone batching beyond V2 while preserving each
variation's insertion architecture, freeze boundary, overflow policy, reconstruction,
and merge behavior. Measure both whole-sort impact and the targeted insertion phase.

Implementation
--------------
- V1: physical-pile version. After the qualifying long initial monotone prefix, scan
  later strict runs. If the first/last values resolve to the same destination physical
  pile, append the whole run to that pile and update its tail/count in bulk; otherwise
  retain scalar insertion.
- V3: reuse V2's E098 simulated continuation directly.
- V4: freeze-aware simulated batching. Batch only inside ranges that preserve the
  existing freeze transition; the power-of-two extension interval remains scalar.
- V5/V6: same freeze-aware structure, but final retained policy additionally requires
  the first continuation run after the qualifying prefix to be >=8 values before the
  natural-run route is enabled. This distinguishes the useful OrganPipe shape from the
  common Rotated shape whose discontinuity is often a one-element bridge.
- V7: propagate E098 into the capped-probe simulated path without changing SIMD probe,
  normalized reconstruction, or merge policy.
- Public V1/V3/V4/V7 routes enable the broad E098 gate. Public V5/V6 routes enable the
  stricter first-continuation-run gate.

Rotated benchmark structure
---------------------------
The canonical Rotated generator is stochastic per trial: it creates [0,1,...,n-1],
samples a split point uniformly from [0,n-1], and calls std::rotate at that point.
Therefore Rotated is not one fixed two-run geometry. The random split directly changes
the initial monotone-prefix fraction and can move a trial across E098's 12.5% activation
boundary. This is a real source of trial-to-trial variability and should be preserved in
benchmarking because it tests different rotation geometries, but it requires enough paired
samples to avoid over-interpreting one split distribution.

Measurement correction
----------------------
Initial same-process A/B showed large, inconsistent first-vs-second-sort effects on
OrganPipe/Rotated. Cache scrubbing did not remove them, indicating allocator/process-state
reuse rather than simple data-cache warmth. Final whole-sort evidence therefore used a
fork-isolated harness: baseline and candidate each run in a fresh child process from the
same parent state with identical input. Direct insertion-phase harnesses were also used
for V3-V7. Correctness required exact equality of blueprint, pile counts, tails, freeze/
overflow metadata where applicable, and final sorted output.

Representative fork-isolated whole-sort ratios (candidate / baseline)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Variation   100k OrganPipe   100k Rotated   1m OrganPipe   1m Rotated
V1             0.7972          0.8685          0.8056         0.8861
V3             0.9829          0.9915          0.9517         0.9644
V4             0.9620          0.9947          0.9670         0.9816
V5*            0.9468          ~1.00           0.9504         ~1.00
V6*            ~0.95           ~1.00           ~0.95          ~1.00
V7             0.9520          0.9966          0.9690         0.9827

* V5/V6 final production policy uses the stricter first-continuation-run gate; earlier
  broad-gate Rotated measurements showed small repeatable regressions and motivated the
  stricter retained condition.

Targeted insertion-phase findings
---------------------------------
- V3-V7 generally show real local improvements even where whole-sort movement is only
  a few percent. At 1m, OrganPipe insertion improvements are typically ~7-10%; Rotated
  improvements are smaller and variation-dependent.
- V1 is the standout propagation target: whole-sort gains remain roughly 19-20% on
  OrganPipe and 11-13% on Rotated because physical-pile insertion avoids a larger amount
  of repeated routing work when a compatible run can be appended in bulk.
- Random is effectively neutral across variations, confirming the long-prefix gate keeps
  the scanner off ordinary high-entropy input.

Decision
--------
RETAIN across V1, V3, V4, V5, V6, and V7. Use the broad E098 gate in V1/V2/V3/V4/V7.
Use the stricter first-continuation-run >=8 gate in V5/V6. Treat Rotated as a randomized
family of split geometries, not a fixed shape, and use high-trial paired/fork-isolated
evidence for future gate changes.

E102 — V3 FINAL PILE-DENSITY ROUTING RECALIBRATION
Date: 2026-08-11
Status: RETAINED, REVISED AFTER HIGH-TRIAL REVALIDATION; V3-specific 4.0 / 4.25 / 5.0 thresholds.

Why this was revisited
----------------------
- The propagation audit found that V3 still used E038's fixed
  `final_piles^2 >= 5*n` branchless-merge trigger, while V2/V7 had later moved to
  E053's size-aware thresholds.
- V3 is non-freezing and shares V2 insertion semantics, but its in-place cycle flattening
  changes run ordering and the production merge context, so its crossover must be measured
  independently.

Original E102 decision and why it was reopened
----------------------------------------------
- The first E102 pass used a forced-kernel merge-only calibration that suggested branchless
  crossover densities near 4.0 at 10k, 4.2-4.3 at 100k, and 5.0 at 1m.
- A smaller set of fork-isolated whole-sort tests initially appeared to show regressions from
  those lower thresholds (roughly 5% at 10k K200 and 15% at 100k K600), so the first E102
  decision conservatively retained 6.0 / 6.5 / 7.0.
- This contradicted a strong phase-local win. Per the lesson from E090, that disagreement was
  reopened and both sides were deliberately over-sampled rather than treating the smaller
  whole-sort result as final.

High-trial revalidation methodology
------------------------------------
- All inputs use E100's canonical base seed and `benchmark_seed.h` trial mapping.
- Isolated merge measurements use identical reconstructed V3 runs and compare forced
  general/galloping versus E085 bidirectional branchless merging.
- Whole-sort measurements use fork-isolated paired child processes from the same parent
  state, comparing the lower candidate policy directly against the then-retained E102
  6.0 / 6.5 / 7.0 policy.
- Correctness validation is required on every child result.
- Trial counts were increased well beyond the original evidence:
    * 10k crossover cases: 10,000 phase pairs + 5,000 whole-sort pairs each.
    * 100k crossover cases: 5,000 phase pairs + 2,000 whole-sort pairs each.
    * 1m crossover cases: 1,000 phase pairs + 400 whole-sort pairs each.
- Pile-density distributions were separately profiled with canonical trials so thresholds
  are tied to density rather than particular Random-K cardinalities.

High-trial results
------------------
10k:
- K150, median density ~3.72: branchless phase ~0.822, but whole sort ~0.999 (neutral).
- K200, median density ~4.41: branchless phase ~0.716; lower-policy whole sort ~0.871.
- K300, median density ~5.20: branchless phase ~0.587; lower-policy whole sort ~0.798.
- Therefore 4.0*n is a conservative useful production threshold: it leaves almost all K150
  trials on general merge while routing K200+ to the clearly faster branchless kernel.

100k:
- K400, median density ~3.42: branchless phase ~1.299 (too early); whole sort ~1.003.
- K500, median density ~4.03: branchless phase ~1.034 (near crossover/slightly early);
  whole sort ~1.000.
- K600, median density ~4.52: branchless phase ~0.908; lower-policy whole sort ~0.962.
- K800, median density ~5.18: branchless phase ~0.743; whole sort ~0.895.
- K1000, median density ~5.60: branchless phase ~0.663; whole sort ~0.846.
- Therefore 4.25*n cleanly separates the K500-like neutral/early regime from the K600+
  regime where the branchless kernel wins materially.

1m:
- K1500, median density ~3.99: branchless phase ~1.249; whole sort ~1.004 (neutral).
- K2000, median density ~4.76: branchless phase ~1.121; whole sort ~0.986 (near neutral).
- K2500, median density ~5.28: branchless phase ~0.961; whole sort ~0.978.
- K3000, median density ~5.65: branchless phase ~0.871; whole sort ~0.973.
- K5000, median density ~6.44: branchless phase ~0.723; whole sort ~0.905.
- Therefore 5.0*n is the appropriate large-N crossover: it excludes the clearly slower
  K1500/K2000 phase regime and admits K2500+ where the local and end-to-end signals improve.

Retained rule (revised E102)
----------------------------
- n <= 20k: branchless if final_piles^2 >= 4*n.
- 20k < n < 500k: branchless if final_piles^2 >= 4.25*n (4*pile^2 >= 17*n).
- n >= 500k: branchless if final_piles^2 >= 5*n.
- Existing `earlyRandomLike`, <=96-byte trivially-copyable merge guard, natural-run route,
  and selected PowerSort scheduling remain unchanged.

Interpretation / process lesson
-------------------------------
- The original isolated calibration was directionally correct. The apparent early
  whole-sort regressions were not stable once both layers were adequately sampled.
- This reproduces the earlier E090 lesson in a different phase: a strong local optimization
  can be diluted or obscured by noisy whole-sort timing, and small conflicting end-to-end
  signals require substantially more evidence before they override a clear causal phase win.
- Future experiments must mark this situation UNRESOLVED and over-sample BOTH phase-local
  and whole-sort measurements before retain/reject decisions.

E103 — TESTING-PROCESS REVISION + REVISIT TAXONOMY
Date: 2026-08-11
Status: RETAINED PROCESS CHANGE; roadmap reprioritized, no sorting logic changed.

Why this experiment/process revision was needed
-----------------------------------------------
- E090 and especially the high-trial E102 revalidation showed the same failure mode:
  a strong, repeatable phase-local improvement was initially overshadowed by a smaller,
  noisier whole-sort signal. When both layers were substantially over-sampled, the local
  win remained and the apparent whole-sort regression reversed into a modest improvement.
- Conversely, E091 and E097 lost badly in the targeted phase itself; more whole-sort trials
  are unlikely to rescue those unchanged mechanisms.
- E101 demonstrated a third category: E063 had once been valid, but E085 changed the
  underlying merge cost model enough that the old routing heuristic became stale.
- Therefore a single REJECTED label is too coarse for a long-running optimization program.

Retained rejection taxonomy
----------------------------
1. MECHANISM REJECTED
   - The targeted phase itself loses materially with adequate trials.
   - Examples: E091 4-ary pile search; E097 mutable Eytzinger tail search; E034 smaller-side
     merge; E013/E014/E015 merge-tree experiments with large direct regressions.
   - Do not retry unchanged unless a materially new mechanism, data representation, compiler,
     or hardware regime changes the premise.

2. INTEGRATION REJECTED
   - The local kernel/mechanism wins, but the attempted way of feeding enough work into it or
     amortizing setup/spill/routing overhead loses. The mechanism remains a valid research
     direction through a different integration.
   - Canonical example: E057 proved exact-eight AVX2 lookup ~23-25% faster locally; E058
     rejected the one-shot probe-gated integration because too little work stayed SIMD-resident.
   - E055 broad natural-run batching is another useful precedent: the broad integration lost,
     while E098/E099 later retained a narrowly gated version of the same underlying idea.

3. POLICY / STALE-DEPENDENT REJECTED
   - A threshold, scheduler, or route loses under the then-current phase costs. Major later
     kernel changes can invalidate the old decision in either direction.
   - Canonical example: E063 was historically beneficial, but E085's bidirectional merge
     reversed the pair-level economics; E101 retired it.
   - Old PowerSort propagation decisions and gallop-trigger routing fall into this class more
     naturally than permanent mechanism rejection.

Mandatory post-retention propagation protocol
---------------------------------------------
- Every retained algorithmic/performance change triggers a propagation audit across V1-V7.
- For each other variation, classify the change as:
    * TESTED + RETAINED,
    * TESTED + REJECTED,
    * NOT APPLICABLE because the relevant phase/data structure does not exist there, or
    * INTENTIONALLY DIFFERENT, with the architectural reason recorded.
- If a plausible propagation test cannot be run in the same experiment, add it explicitly to
  Section 2 before starting unrelated optimization work. Do not rely on memory or on shared
  helper code to imply that propagation has happened.
- Propagation should preserve each variation's defining architecture. Port the mechanism, not
  unrelated V2/V7 implementation details, and use causal A/B evidence independently per
  variation when the economics can differ.
- Once propagation is resolved, update both the detailed E### record and the current-state
  summary so the retained family-wide scope is unambiguous.

Mandatory local-vs-whole disagreement protocol
----------------------------------------------
- If a causal phase benchmark shows a strong repeatable improvement but whole-sort timing
  shows only a small regression, neutral result, or unstable opposite signal, mark the
  experiment UNRESOLVED.
- Increase trial counts substantially on BOTH layers before retain/reject. Trial escalation
  should be proportional to the effect size; E102 used up to 10,000 phase pairs / 5,000
  whole-sort pairs at 10k, 5,000 / 2,000 at 100k, and 1,000 / 400 at 1m.
- Preserve E100's canonical seed population and trial mapping so the two layers observe the
  same input distribution.
- Use fork-isolated paired whole-sort timing when allocator/process-state ordering effects
  remain visible after ordinary alternation.
- Report both local phase ratio and whole-sort ratio. Amdahl dilution is expected and is not
  itself evidence against a local optimization.
- Conversely, when the targeted phase itself loses decisively with adequate trials, do not
  consume large whole-sort budgets trying to rescue the unchanged mechanism.

Revisit audit after E102
------------------------
HIGH PRIORITY
- V3 E045 gallop trigger: RESOLVED by E104. High-trial revalidation reversed E048's earlier
  lightly sampled rejection and retained the 5/7 selector in V3. Propagation is complete: V1,
  V2, V3, and V7 retain the selector; V4 qualifying shapes are already diverted to PowerSort;
  V5/V6 frozen overflow layouts do not meet the selector's 12-128 balanced-run gate in the
  propagation scan.
- SIMD V7+ repeated/chunked integration: E057's local kernel win is proven; E058 rejected only
  one integration strategy. Test sustained/chunked SIMD residency rather than rerunning the
  old one-shot probe unchanged.

MEDIUM / CONDITIONAL
- V5 dynamic overflow + merge-tree balancing: do not rerun E060/E062's crude heuristics; build
  the stronger joint overflow/scheduler cost model already on the roadmap. The old low-trial
  simple-policy failures do not close the broader mechanism.
- PowerSort in V1/V5/V6: E024-E026 had meaningful regressions and should remain closed as
  standalone reruns, but classify them as stale-dependent because E085 materially changed
  merge costs. Revisit only if merge-tree work is active, preferably jointly with new shaping.

KEEP CLOSED UNCHANGED
- E091 4-ary pile search: direct target phase 2.6-3.4x slower over thousands of trials.
- E097 mutable Eytzinger search: target insertion up to ~72% slower and whole sort ~24% slower.
- E034 smaller-side merge, E013/E014/E015 merge-tree alternatives: broad/large regressions.
- E094 hot/cold merge continuation: already 5,000/2,000/400 phase trials and essentially 1.0.
- E056 scalar hint-miss tweaks: the apparent win disappeared after restoring production-like
  code layout and there is no retained strong local-kernel win to rescue.

Roadmap decision
----------------
- E104 resolves the V3 E045 revisit and its propagation audit.
- E105 resolves the immediate repeated/chunked AVX2 revisit: retain only the narrow 5k-20k
  low-pile one-register continuation; broader/global and large-n integration remain rejected.
- Keep V5 tree-aware overflow work as the highest-priority active optimization.
- Revalidate old PowerSort propagation only when a new merge-tree design makes the question
  relevant again.
- Keep V1 density recalibration and first-merge-pass fusion active after these revisit items.

Validation / repository impact
------------------------------
- E103 changes experiment methodology, status semantics, and roadmap only.
- No sorting implementation, benchmark workload generator, or retained threshold changed.
- Section 1 timing methodology and Section 2 active roadmap were updated to make this process
  authoritative for future experiments, including mandatory post-retention propagation audits.

E105 — V7 REPEATED ONE-REGISTER AVX2 CONTINUATION
Date: 2026-08-11
Status: RETAINED NARROWLY IN V7; BROADER INTEGRATION REJECTED; PROPAGATION RESOLVED

Question
- E057 proved that an exact-eight int32 AVX2 pile lookup is ~23-25% faster than the scalar
  bit-walk in isolation, while E058/E075 showed that one-shot probe gating or long capped probes
  do not amortize reliably. Can the same one-register primitive be reused repeatedly inside a
  larger tail array without sequentially scanning multiple vectors or carrying large spill runs?

Candidate design
- After the ordinary exact-hint check misses, compare one contiguous 8-tail AVX2 window centered
  around the previous pile hint. If the insertion boundary lies inside that window, resolve it
  with one vector compare + movemask + ctz; otherwise fall back immediately to the existing
  scalar bit-walk. No sequential multi-register scan is performed.
- Instrumentation showed that global enablement is wrong: at n=100k, Random/Random%100 produced
  thousands of window attempts but only ~8-10% window hits, whereas Sawtooth produced only ~173
  attempts but ~94% of those attempts resolved inside the window. The ordinary exact hint already
  catches the overwhelming majority of Sawtooth insertions, limiting the maximum whole-sort gain.
- A free outer gate therefore reuses post-probe structure: enable the window only when the input
  size is 5k-20k and the two ordinary post-probe pile counts sum to <=12. No new scan or per-input
  generator identity is used.

High-trial evidence
- Ungated 100k integration was rejected: Random insertion slowed by roughly 1%, confirming that
  low-hit vector probes cannot be paid globally.
- Gated 100k Sawtooth initially produced only a tiny benefit and 1m reversed: 1m Sawtooth over
  1,200 fork-isolated insertion-phase pairs was ~0.43% slower and 400 whole-sort pairs were
  ~0.31% slower. Therefore large-n windowing is rejected.
- 10k Sawtooth, targeted small-size route:
    * insertion phase: 5,000 fork-isolated pairs, window/baseline median 0.98393
      (~1.6% faster);
    * whole sort: initial 3,000-pair run median 0.99285 (~0.7% faster);
    * independent 3,000-pair replication using later canonical trial indices: 0.99267
      (~0.73% faster).
- 20k Sawtooth:
    * insertion phase: 4,000 pairs, 0.99295 (~0.7% faster);
    * whole sort: 2,500 pairs, 0.99769 (~0.23% faster).
- 10k full-workload guardrail, 2,000 fork-isolated whole-sort pairs each: Random 1.00023,
  Sorted+Noise 0.99974, Random%100 1.00183, Alternating 0.99998, Sawtooth 0.99598,
  BlockSorted 1.00002, OrganPipe 1.00000, Rotated 0.99885. The small Random%100 movement
  did not replicate: an independent 3,000-pair run was 1.00099 (neutral).
- n=1k Sawtooth was neutral/slightly negative (1.00074 over 5,000 pairs), motivating the lower
  5k size gate rather than enabling the route for all small arrays.

Decision
- RETAIN only the narrow V7 route: int/std::less AVX2, n in [5k,20k], <=12 ordinary post-probe
  piles, and only after the scalar exact-hint check misses.
- REJECT global windowing, 100k+ continuation, and any sequential multi-register AVX2 scan.
- E057 remains a valid mechanism win; E105 shows that its useful integration envelope is small
  because the existing hint path already removes most structured-search work.

Mandatory propagation audit
- V7: TESTED + RETAINED narrowly.
- V1-V6: INTENTIONALLY NOT PROPAGATED. This is an int/std::less AVX2 specialization and V7 exists
  specifically as the SIMD research variation; copying it into V2 would erase the scalar V2
  comparison baseline, while V1/V3-V6 have distinct insertion representations. Revisit only if
  SIMD becomes part of the intended production/default family rather than an experimental V7+ axis.

Validation
- Repository rebuilt cleanly with -O3 -march=native -DNDEBUG -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic.
- Standard 100-trial x 10k x 10-workload benchmark completed with correctness validation across
  all V1-V7 variations after enabling the retained V7 route.

Process interpretation
- This is an INTEGRATION result, not a new SIMD mechanism result. The high-trial process avoided
  both errors: global/large-n integration was rejected from direct phase evidence, while the
  small 10k local win was not discarded merely because its whole-sort gain was sub-1%.

E106 — V5 DATA-AWARE FIRST OVERFLOW-BOUNDARY / MERGE-TREE SHAPING
Date: 2026-08-11
Status: REJECTED; NO PRODUCTION CODE CHANGE; V5 RESTORED BYTE-FOR-BYTE

Question
- E060 found that local boundary shaping was safer than global adaptive band sizing, and E062
  concluded that overflow shaping has useful leverage primarily when it changes merge levels,
  enables ordered-boundary elimination, or is paired with a size-aware scheduler. Can V5 use
  actual reconstructed values at the frozen/overflow boundary to shape only the first deferred
  band while leaving all later bands at the retained size 32?

Candidate A — split 32 -> 16 only to create an ordered boundary
- After ordinary V5 reconstruction gathers overflow values, inspect the maximum of the last
  frozen run plus prefix minima of the first 16 and 32 overflow values.
- Trigger only when sorting the first 16 values would make the frozen/overflow boundary already
  globally ordered, while the ordinary 32-value first band would not. Use 16 only for that first
  band, then immediately return to canonical 32-element bands.
- 100k standard-workload screening showed meaningful triggers only on Sorted+Noise (~8% of
  canonical trials); other standard families almost never qualified.
- Trigger-conditioned 100k Sorted+Noise, canonical seeds:
    * 1,000 reconstruction+merge phase pairs: candidate/baseline median 1.00306;
    * 500 fork-isolated whole-sort pairs: 1.00016.
- Decision: reject. Creating one eliminable boundary by splitting the first band is neutral to
  slightly slower and does not repay the changed small-sort/run geometry.

Candidate B — coalesce first ordered region to 64
- Opposite strategy: if all first 64 overflow values can form a run already globally ordered
  after the last frozen run, make the first deferred overflow run size 64 and then return to 32.
  This preserves an eliminable frozen/overflow boundary while reducing downstream run count.
- Initial 1,000 phase / 500 whole-sort trigger-conditioned pairs disagreed slightly, so E103's
  mandatory high-trial rule was invoked.
- Expanded 100k Sorted+Noise evidence:
    * 4,000 reconstruction+merge phase pairs: 0.99667 (~0.33% faster locally);
    * 1,600 fork-isolated whole-sort pairs: 1.00289 (~0.29% slower end-to-end).
- The local gain is small and the actual sort is consistently worse at higher trial count.
  Decision: reject this integration.

Candidate C — coalesce first ordered region to 128
- Same data-aware condition as Candidate B but require the first 128 overflow values to remain
  globally ordered after the last frozen run, reducing more overflow runs at the price of a
  larger deferred sort.
- Expanded trigger-conditioned 100k Sorted+Noise evidence:
    * 3,000 reconstruction+merge phase pairs: 1.00063 (neutral);
    * 1,200 fork-isolated whole-sort pairs: 1.00265 (~0.27% slower).
- Decision: reject.

Interpretation
- E106 strengthens E062's structural conclusion. Under V5's current scheduler, changing only the
  first overflow boundary does not provide enough downstream leverage, even when the planner uses
  actual values and explicitly preserves/creates an ordered boundary.
- The 32-element E090 smallsort plus current adjacent/defer scheduler is already difficult to beat
  with a single local boundary perturbation.
- Future V5 merge-tree work should therefore require a materially different idea: jointly plan
  multiple overflow boundaries and/or select a scheduler from actual post-insertion structure.
  Do not retry fixed/global band constants, blind pair equalization, power-of-two run counts, or
  first-boundary-only 16/64/128 shaping unchanged.

Research-process handling
- Candidate B produced the exact local-vs-whole disagreement covered by E103. Neither layer was
  allowed to decide the experiment from the initial sample; both were substantially oversampled.
- Because no candidate was retained, the mandatory post-retention propagation audit does not
  apply. V1-V4/V6/V7 remain unchanged.

Repository result
- All E106 experimental hooks/harnesses were removed from the packaged repository.
- include/jessesort/v5_deferred_bands.h was restored byte-for-byte to its E105 production version.
- The repository rebuilds cleanly after restoration.

- /mnt/data/e106_trigger64_100k_hi.csv
- /mnt/data/e106_trigger128_100k_hi2.csv

E108 — V1/V2/V3 NON-FREEZING PARITY AUDIT + RECONCILIATION ROADMAP
Date: 2026-08-11
Status: DOCUMENTATION / PLANNING; NO SORTING LOGIC CHANGED

Trigger
- E107 retained V1 density thresholds of 3.5/4.25/5.0 while V2 still used E053's
  6.0/6.75/7.5 and V3 used revised E102's 4.0/4.25/5.0.
- This divergence was questioned because V1/V2/V3 are all non-freezing JesseSort designs and
  should form the same patience piles for the same input. V4-V6 can legitimately diverge
  because freeze/overflow changes final run geometry; V7 can diverge because its capped-probe
  path can alter pile formation. The non-freezing trio requires a stricter explanation.

Code audit findings
1. V2 and V3 insertion/pile formation are identical by construction.
   - V3 calls `jessesort::simulated::simulatePatienceInsertionBlueprint()` directly.
   - Therefore game assignment, local pile ID, asc/desc pile counts, and per-pile lengths are
     identical for V2 and V3 before flattening.
2. V1 is a separate physical-pile implementation but intends the same pile-placement semantics.
   - It owns independent search/hint code and physical vectors, so semantic parity should be
     asserted directly rather than assumed forever.
   - Nothing in its intended algorithm requires different pile counts or lengths.
3. Materialized game-group order differs.
   - V1: descending-game piles first, then ascending-game piles.
   - V2: descending-game runs first, then ascending-game runs.
   - V3: ascending-game runs first, then descending-game runs.
   - This does NOT change the run-length multiset, but it changes adjacency and therefore can
     alter pairings, ordered boundaries, PowerSort decisions, and cache state.
4. Merge-driver implementation differs materially.
   - V1 owns `mergeRuns()`.
   - V2/V3 share `mergeRunsFromTmpToArr()`.
   - V1 and V2/V3 therefore should not be assigned different thresholds before testing whether
     driver differences themselves are still justified.
5. Scheduler policy differs materially.
   - V2/V3 can call `shouldUsePowerSortMerge()` / `mergeRunsPowerSortStyle()`.
   - V1 currently has no corresponding PowerSort path.
   - This is a major confound because scheduler choice changes merge depth/traffic and which
     adjacent pairs reach the two-run kernels.
6. Ordered-boundary probing differs.
   - V1 removes already ordered adjacent boundaries every merge level.
   - Shared V2/V3 initially probes boundaries but adaptively disables the scan when it ceases
     to remove enough boundaries (`newRuns > 64` and <25% removed).
7. Random-regime classifier differs.
   - V1: final density + minority-game >=25% balance gate.
   - V2/V3: `earlyRandomLike` insertion signal + final density.
   - Thus two variations with the same pile shape can intentionally make different routing
     decisions before the density coefficient is even considered.
8. Flatten/reconstruction memory path differs.
   - V1 physically flattens into a new `flat` vector.
   - V2 counting-scatters normalized blueprint IDs into temporary storage.
   - V3 cycle-permutes the input in place, then prepares merge ping-pong storage.
   - These can affect cache/allocation state but do not justify assuming different pile shape.

Threshold interpretation correction
- Current coefficients remain production behavior for now, but their interpretation changes:

Canonical game-numbering hypothesis

  V1/V2's natural materialized order is descending then ascending.
- Proposed test: renumber descending game = 0 and ascending game = 1, then use one canonical

  order logic, and a cleaner shared non-freezing pipeline.

  model; that result must be revalidated after E085/E093/E102/E104 rather than ignored or assumed

A. Build an insertion-shape parity harness and prove V1/V2/V3 pile equivalence directly.
B. Isolate game numbering/materialization order. Re-test V3 ascending-first vs descending-first

H. Defer first-merge-pass fusion until this baseline is reconciled so fusion work is not built on
   accidental V1/V2/V3 drift.

Decision
- NO CODE CHANGE.
- Reframe the current V1/V2/V3 differences as an explicit reconciliation program and make it the
  top of the active roadmap.
- The next experiment should be the insertion-shape parity harness, not another density tweak.

E110 — V1/V2/V3 MATERIALIZED GAME/RUN ORDER REVALIDATION
Date: 2026-08-11
Status: REJECTED AS COMMON CHANGE; E109 DESCENDING-FIRST BASELINE RETAINED

Question
- E109 standardized V1/V2/V3 on descending-game runs first, then ascending-game runs.
- V3 historically used ascending-first, and E109's bundle showed a large V3 OrganPipe regression.
- Test whether ascending-first should replace descending-first across the entire non-freezing trio.
- Per the E109 process rule, the candidate was implemented and measured in V1, V2, and V3 rather
  than restoring it only to its historical owner.

Candidate
- V1: flatten ascending physical piles first, then reversed descending piles.
- V2: normalized simulated reconstruction assigns ascending runs the low global run IDs and
  descending runs the high IDs; cursor direction remains correct for each game.
- V3: in-place destination layout becomes ascending runs first, then descending runs.
- No pile formation, density routing, merge kernel, PowerSort policy, or boundary-probe policy was
  intentionally changed.

Correctness
- Candidate rebuilt successfully.
- 10-workload smoke benchmark passed sortedness validation for V1-V7.

Measurement
- 50 deterministic paired trials per workload at 10k and 100k, same benchmark seed policy.
- Deltas below are candidate ascending-first median paired ratio to std::sort divided by the E109
  descending-first baseline median paired ratio; negative is faster with ascending-first.

10k notable deltas
- V1: BlockSorted -10.1%, Random%100 -1.7%, but OrganPipe +9.0%, Rotated +16.1%,
  and Alternating +44.7%. Other workloads were near neutral to small regressions.
- V2: Sorted+Noise -5.4%, Rotated -6.4%; OrganPipe +4.4%, Alternating +3.5%, Random%100 +2.6%.
  Overall median effect was essentially neutral.
- V3: OrganPipe -14.4%, BlockSorted -6.7%, Sawtooth -2.8%; Rotated +15.6%, Alternating +4.0%.
  This confirms that V3's E109 OrganPipe loss was substantially caused by materialized order, but
  the historical ascending-first order introduces a meaningful Rotated tradeoff under current code.

100k notable deltas
- V1: Alternating -7.2%; BlockSorted +5.1%, Sorted+Noise +3.1%, OrganPipe +2.0%.
  Rotated measured much faster (-46.5%), opposite the 10k direction, reinforcing that order effects
  are strongly workload/size dependent rather than a stable global V1 win.
- V2: Alternating -10.0% and Rotated -30.3%; OrganPipe +9.9%, Random%100 +7.7%, Random and
  Sorted+Noise +3.8% each. Again, no broad common win.
- V3: OrganPipe -18.7%, Sawtooth -3.9%, BlockSorted -2.1%; Rotated +8.2%, Sorted+Noise +5.7%,
  Random +3.1%.

Interpretation
- Materialized game order is not performance-neutral under the current schedulers: changing which
  game appears first changes adjacent run boundaries and therefore merge scheduling/traffic.
- V3 has a real architecture-specific reason to prefer ascending-first on OrganPipe and some other
  structured layouts, but that preference is not universal even within V3 because Rotated moves in
  the opposite direction.
- V1 and V2 likewise show large workload/size interactions. Therefore a single global order policy
  cannot be justified from this test, and restoring V3's old order unconditionally would simply
  trade one regression family for another.

Decision
- REJECT ascending-first as a common V1/V2/V3 baseline change.
- Restore and retain E109 descending-first for all three for now.
- Preserve the measured V3 OrganPipe/BlockSorted/Sawtooth recovery as evidence for a possible future
  structure-aware order/scheduler interaction test after the simpler E109 policies are isolated.
- Continue the policy-centric reconciliation sequence; next candidate should test another E109
  convergence policy across all three rather than making a V3-only exception from this mixed result.

1m guardrail
- V1 strongly favored the ends driver overall, including Sawtooth (~15.8% faster), BlockSorted
  (~10.6%), OrganPipe (~20.1%), and Rotated (~39.3%); Random remained ~6.3% faster with run-start.
- V2 was mixed and slightly favored run-start in aggregate (~1.4%), with ends notably faster on
  Alternating (~16.7%) but slower on Random (~5.0%) and Sawtooth (~5.4%).
- V3 was approximately aggregate-neutral (~0.3% in favor of ends), with ends faster on Random,
  Alternating, Sawtooth, and Rotated but slower on BlockSorted and OrganPipe.
- Across all three variations and all ten workloads, summed median time was ~2.34% lower with the
  ends-based driver, essentially matching the 100k aggregate result.

Decision
- RETAIN the V1 ends-based adjacent-pair merge driver as the single common V1/V2/V3 post-flatten
  implementation.
- Move that implementation into the shared simulated namespace and call it from V1, V2, and V3.
- REMOVE V1's duplicate private merge-driver implementation and remove the temporary A/B switches.
- V2/V3 convert their run-start metadata to run ends before entering the common driver; V1 already
  has run ends naturally.
- The common driver retains the reconciled E109/E111/E112 policies: adaptive ordered-boundary
  probing, adjacent-pair scheduling, E045 gallop-trigger selection, and the current branchless merge
  routing/kernel choices.
- V4+ are not changed by E115; their merge policies remain separately validated.
